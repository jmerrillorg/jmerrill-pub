# jmp_cover_production_specs
J Merrill Publishing Cover Production Specifications
Status: Recreated for the current Cover Intelligence GPT (August 2026). Not
a recovery of a prior artifact — built intentionally around the present
system. Attach as a Knowledge file to the JMP Cover Intelligence GPT.

Purpose: Provide production-level rules for preparing JMP cover assets
across print, ebook, and audiobook editions.

---

## 1. Governing Principle

Final technical specifications must come from the actual distributor,
printer, or platform being used for that specific edition. Do not
substitute generic production assumptions when a current title-specific
template is available.

---

## 2. Standard JMP Print Trim

Unless project specifications state otherwise: default trim size is 6 x 9
inches. Other trim sizes may be used where appropriate to the title,
format, genre, contract, or production requirement.

---

## 3. Print Distribution Routing

JMP uses a tiered print-distribution model.

**Standard Print Titles** — default routing:
JMP → IngramSpark → Ingram distribution network → retail/library/
institutional channels

**Higher-Marketability / Higher-Volume Titles** — where anticipated demand
or strategic value warrants it:
JMP → Lightning Source → Ingram distribution network → retail/library/
institutional channels

Distribution path must be known before final cover production. Do not
finalize a print wrap against the wrong distributor template.

---

## 4. IngramSpark Print Covers

For an IngramSpark title: obtain the title-specific IngramSpark cover
template. Use the confirmed ISBN, trim size, binding, paper type, page
count, and bleed settings. Build the full wrap against the generated
template. Use the template's calculated spine dimensions. Preserve barcode
and safety zones.

Never estimate final spine width when the official template can be
generated.

---

## 5. Lightning Source Print Covers

For a Lightning Source title: obtain the title-specific Lightning Source
template. Confirm ISBN, trim, binding, page count, paper stock,
color/interior type, production configuration. Follow current LSI
technical specifications. Use the official template as the final
dimensional authority.

Do not assume IngramSpark and Lightning Source production files are
interchangeable simply because both operate within the Ingram ecosystem.

---

## 6. Bleed

Unless the applicable printer template specifies otherwise: standard bleed
allowance is 0.125 inch on applicable outer edges. Artwork intended to
reach the trim edge must extend fully through the bleed area. Do not place
critical text or faces inside bleed-sensitive zones.

---

## 7. Safe Areas

Keep critical text, logos, faces, and essential design details inside the
printer-defined safety areas. This includes: title, subtitle, author,
spine text, back-cover copy, logo, endorsements, essential artwork.

Official template guides take precedence over generic safe-margin
assumptions.

---

## 8. Spine Width

Spine width is not fixed. It depends on: page count, paper stock, binding,
printer specification, interior configuration. Use the applicable
IngramSpark or Lightning Source template/calculator. Do not calculate from
trim size alone.

---

## 9. Print Resolution

Raster imagery used for final print production should normally be 300 DPI
at final reproduction size. Do not artificially upscale low-resolution
assets and treat them as native high-resolution art without quality
review.

---

## 10. Color Management

Final print cover production should follow the current color requirements
of the selected printer/distributor.

As a production principle: design with print reproduction in mind, avoid
colors that depend heavily on screen-only luminosity, review dark areas
for loss of detail, review highly saturated colors for print shift. Use
distributor-specific ICC/color-profile guidance when required.

---

## 11. Laminate / Finish Consideration

Cover finish affects perceived color and contrast.

**Matte** — often supports: literary, premium nonfiction, memoir, poetry,
sophisticated or restrained presentation. Potential concern: some colors
may appear slightly more subdued.

**Gloss** — often supports: vivid imagery, children's books, highly
saturated commercial art, photography requiring visual pop. Potential
concern: reflectivity may alter perceived presentation under direct light.

Finish recommendations should be project-specific.

---

## 12. Paperback Covers

Required production asset: full wrap PDF (back cover, spine, front cover).
Use distributor template dimensions.

---

## 13. Hardcover Covers

Do not assume paperback dimensions can be reused. Hardcover production may
involve: case laminate, jacketed hardcover, different board dimensions,
wrap allowances, flaps, different spine calculations.

Use the exact title-specific hardcover template.

---

## 14. Ebook Cover Production

JMP ebooks are distributed through:
JMP → CoreSource → downstream ebook retail, library, institutional, and
other enabled digital channels

Ebook cover requirements should be prepared to CoreSource's current
accepted specifications. The ebook cover should: use front-cover artwork
only, remain readable at thumbnail size, maintain the approved
print-edition visual identity, avoid crop-dependent elements near outer
edges.

Always verify current CoreSource requirements before final delivery.

---

## 15. Audiobook Cover Production

JMP currently uses:
JMP → CoreSource → downstream audiobook channels

Audiobook artwork must be created as a square edition-specific cover.
Requirements: 1:1 aspect ratio, high-resolution square source, strong
title readability at small size, simplified composition where necessary,
visual continuity with print and ebook editions.

Do not simply crop a rectangular cover if doing so compromises hierarchy
or composition. Verify current CoreSource audiobook-art specifications
before final delivery.

---

## 16. Digital Master Principle

Where possible, retain layered/master source files so JMP can generate:
paperback wrap, hardcover wrap, ebook front cover, audiobook square cover,
retail promotional derivatives, social media graphics, future revised
editions.

Do not treat the flattened print PDF as the only archival master.

---

## 17. File Naming

Use controlled naming conventions.

Recommended pattern: JMP_[TitleSlug]_[Format]_[Version]_[YYYYMMDD]

Examples:
- JMP_BookTitle_PB_Cover_v03_20260819.pdf
- JMP_BookTitle_EBOOK_Cover_v02_20260819.jpg
- JMP_BookTitle_AUDIO_Cover_v01_20260819.jpg

Avoid: final-final, new-final, use-this-one, arbitrary filenames.

---

## 18. Preflight Checklist

Before submitting any cover, confirm: title spelling, subtitle spelling,
author name, imprint, ISBN, edition/format, trim, page count, paper type,
distributor path, official template, spine width, bleed, safe zones,
barcode area, image resolution, text contrast, logo treatment, latest
approved cover version, correct export format.

---

## 19. Cover Production Killers

Flag immediately: wrong distributor template, wrong trim size, wrong page
count, estimated spine used as final, low-resolution imagery, essential
content outside safe zones, barcode collision, text placed over
high-detail imagery without adequate contrast, unapproved logo
modification, missing edition-specific cover derivative, accidental
RGB/screen-dependent output where printer requirements differ, untracked
or ambiguous final-version filenames.

---

## 20. Final Authority

The final technical authority for any edition is, in order:
1. Current JMP-approved project specifications
2. Current title-specific distributor/printer template
3. Current distributor technical documentation
4. JMP production standards

If these conflict, the conflict must be surfaced before final cover
release rather than guessed around.
