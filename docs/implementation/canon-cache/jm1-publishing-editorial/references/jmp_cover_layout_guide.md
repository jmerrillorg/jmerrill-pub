# jmp_cover_layout_guide
J Merrill Publishing Cover Layout Guide
Status: Recreated for the current Cover Intelligence GPT (August 2026). Not
a recovery of a prior artifact — built intentionally around the present
system. Attach as a Knowledge file to the JMP Cover Intelligence GPT.

Purpose: Define JMP's standard approach to front-cover, spine, back-cover,
ebook, and audiobook layout development.

---

## 1. Standard Trim Assumption

Unless project specifications state otherwise: default JMP print trim size
is 6 x 9 inches. Never substitute another trim size without confirmation
or project data. Final cover dimensions must always come from the
applicable printer/distributor template for the specific title.

---

## 2. Front Cover Hierarchy

Typical hierarchy: 1) Title, 2) Primary visual or concept, 3) Subtitle if
applicable, 4) Author name, 5) Tagline, endorsement, series identifier, or
descriptor where appropriate.

Genre conventions may justify a different order.

The cover should communicate effectively at: full print size, typical
online retail size, small thumbnail size.

---

## 3. Title Treatment

Evaluate: font category, weight, scale, case, line breaks, tracking,
placement, contrast, interaction with imagery.

The title should remain recognizable at approximately 80px-wide thumbnail
presentation wherever practical.

Avoid: excessive effects, weak contrast, text crossing visually complex
focal areas, forced line breaks that damage meaning.

---

## 4. Subtitle Treatment

If a subtitle exists, determine whether it belongs on the front cover.
Consider: genre, subtitle length, sales value, author platform, available
visual space, whether the subtitle clarifies the book's promise.

For nonfiction, the subtitle frequently carries significant marketing
value and may require prominent placement. For fiction, a subtitle may be
unnecessary unless it is part of the formal title or series identity.

---

## 5. Author Name

Author-name prominence should reflect: genre convention, author
recognition, contractual requirements, series consistency, retail
positioning.

Do not automatically make an unknown author's name compete with the
title. For established or platform-driven authors, stronger prominence may
be appropriate.

---

## 6. Typography System

Each cover direction should identify: title font category, subtitle font
category, author-name treatment, supporting font, pairing rationale.

Prefer a disciplined type system:
- Use no more than 2-3 font families unless the concept specifically
  requires more.
- Do not sacrifice readability for novelty.
- Avoid fonts whose visual language signals the wrong genre.

---

## 7. Front-Cover Visual Composition

Identify: primary focal point, secondary focal point, eye movement,
negative space, text-safe zones, subject placement, relationship between
visual and typography.

The image and typography should function as one design, not two unrelated
layers.

---

## 8. Spine Layout

The spine may include: title, author, JMP imprint/logo, series identifier
where applicable.

Actual spine width must be calculated from: final page count, trim size,
paper type, printer/distributor specifications. Never assume a fixed spine
width.

If the spine is too narrow for readable text or branding, omit elements
rather than forcing illegible content.

---

## 9. Back Cover Layout

Typical back-cover elements: hook or headline, back-cover description,
author bio, author photograph if used, endorsement or testimonial,
publisher imprint/logo, website or approved publisher information, barcode
area.

Maintain sufficient whitespace. Do not crowd the barcode zone.

---

## 10. Back Cover Copy Structure

Recommended structure:
- **Hook** — one strong opening sentence or short paragraph.
- **Body** — explain the book's promise, tension, transformation, or
  subject in concise reader-facing language.
- **Close** — end with an emotional invitation, benefit statement,
  question, or call to engage with the book.

If provided copy is too long or weak for the available layout, flag it
before final production.

---

## 11. Barcode Zone

Reserve the printer/distributor-required barcode area. Do not place
critical imagery, author faces, logo, important text, or decorative
elements required for visual meaning inside the reserved barcode zone.

---

## 12. JMP Logo Placement

The J Merrill Publishing logo should normally appear on the back cover and
may appear on the spine when space allows.

Preferred treatment: bottom back-cover region, visually separated from
primary sales copy, appropriate approved logo variant based on background.

Do not: distort, stretch, arbitrarily recolor, place over visually noisy
areas, make the logo compete with the book itself.

Logo integration occurs during final layout unless otherwise requested.

---

## 13. Full-Wrap Composition

For print editions, treat back + spine + front as one continuous design
surface. Consider: background continuity, image wrapping, texture
continuity, spine transition, visual balance across the spread.

Do not allow important front-cover subjects or text to cross trim or spine
boundaries unintentionally.

---

## 14. Ebook Cover

Ebook covers use the front cover only. Prioritize: thumbnail performance,
simple hierarchy, strong contrast, clean typography, reduced dependence on
fine detail.

The ebook cover should remain visually aligned with the print edition.

---

## 15. Audiobook Cover

When an audiobook edition exists, create a square adaptation of the
approved cover system. The audio cover should: preserve title identity,
preserve recognizable imagery or visual language, simplify elements as
needed, remain highly legible at small app-thumbnail sizes, avoid
attempting to reproduce the entire print front cover without adaptation.

Treat audiobook art as an edition-specific derivative, not merely a crop.

---

## 16. Series Layout Architecture

Series covers should maintain repeatable elements such as: title position,
series marker, typography, author-name placement, spine system, logo
position, image treatment.

Controlled differences can include: dominant color, central imagery,
volume number, subtitle, thematic symbol.

---

## 17. Cover Readability Check

Before approval, assess: title readability, subtitle readability,
author-name readability, thumbnail recognition, grayscale contrast, focal
hierarchy, visual crowding, genre recognition.

Flag any design whose typography becomes unreadable against its imagery.

---

## 18. Revision Protocol

When feedback is provided:
1. Identify the requested change.
2. Separate it into: Composition / Imagery / Character / Color /
   Typography / Mood / Layout / Production specification.
3. Preserve all approved or successful elements.
4. Change only what needs revision unless the user requests a full
   redesign.

Do not restart an established concept unnecessarily.
