# Human-First Presentation Standard

Last Verified: 2026-08-29T12:04:54Z

Internal systems may carry precise workflow state, IDs, scores, diagnostic details, and routing decisions. Author-facing communication must translate that truth into useful publishing language.

Routine author messages should prefer:

- greeting
- short contextual opening
- current truthful state
- one request only when genuinely required
- clear next step
- natural closing

Structured headings remain appropriate for substantive author packages, editorial recommendations, production proofs, rights follow-up, and commercial/package decisions where structure helps the author understand or act.

Guarded terms/patterns:

- raw Dataverse or execution-log language
- correlation IDs and artifact IDs unless directly useful
- workflow/status-report scaffolding for routine notes
- raw scorecards or numeric internal scores
- package routing phrases such as "short enough for Starter"

Evidence Source: `findInternalLanguage`, `validatePackageRecommendation`, brand guard, and Human-First validation tests.
