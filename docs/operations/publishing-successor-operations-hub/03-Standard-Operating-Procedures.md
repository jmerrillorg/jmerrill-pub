# 03 Standard Operating Procedures

These SOPs are business-first. Use the System Support Reference at the end of each SOP only when a support owner needs to find the governed source or underlying proof.

## 1. How to Review a New Publishing Inquiry

Purpose: Decide whether an inquiry is ready for publishing review.

When to Use: A new author, book, partner, or referral asks about publishing.

Who Owns It: Publishing Coordinator.

What You Need Before Starting: Author name, contact details, book title or working title, manuscript status, requested service, deadline, and referral source.

Steps:

1. Confirm the inquiry is for J Merrill Publishing.
2. Capture the author’s plain-language goal.
3. Identify whether a manuscript exists and whether it is complete.
4. Note requested formats, timing, and known concerns.
5. Do not promise acceptance, price, publication date, or package fit.
6. Prepare a short intake summary for Jackie.

Jackie Approval Required? Yes, for acceptance, decline, track, price exception, or special terms.

Evidence to Preserve: Inquiry, intake notes, attachments received, promised follow-up date.

What Good Looks Like: Jackie can understand the opportunity without rereading every message.

When to Stop: Stop if rights, privacy, payment, legal, or reputational concerns appear.

Related Forms and Templates: New inquiry intake summary; status update email.

System Support Reference: Use the active-title register and current intake records named in the Current Authority Index.

## 2. How to Accept or Decline a Publishing Project

Purpose: Record the business decision to move forward or decline.

When to Use: Jackie has reviewed a publishing opportunity.

Who Owns It: President & CEO.

What You Need Before Starting: Intake summary, manuscript status, author goals, track recommendation, known risks, and commercial fit.

Steps:

1. Present the opportunity and recommendation to Jackie.
2. Confirm accept, decline, or hold.
3. If accepted, confirm track and next author action.
4. If declined, prepare respectful closure language.
5. If held, state exactly what is missing.
6. Preserve the decision and any conditions.

Jackie Approval Required? Yes.

Evidence to Preserve: Decision, rationale, conditions, author communication.

What Good Looks Like: The author relationship is handled clearly and the record shows why the title did or did not proceed.

When to Stop: Stop if the decision would require special legal, rights, or financial review.

Related Forms and Templates: Acceptance note; decline note; intake summary.

System Support Reference: Use the active-title register and agreement template index.

## 3. How to Select the Correct Publishing Track

Purpose: Match the title to the correct business relationship.

When to Use: A title is accepted or a track question arises.

Who Owns It: President & CEO.

What You Need Before Starting: Author goals, package or relationship type, payment posture, included services, author-copy benefits, rights expectations, and title scope.

Steps:

1. Identify whether the relationship is Hybrid or JM Signature.
2. Confirm whether package benefits apply.
3. Confirm what the author pays, if anything.
4. Confirm what J Merrill Publishing provides.
5. Confirm whether any custom terms are requested.
6. Record the track decision before issuing an agreement.

Jackie Approval Required? Yes.

Evidence to Preserve: Track decision and any exceptions.

What Good Looks Like: The agreement path is obvious and no one manually chooses a template by preference.

When to Stop: Stop if the track would change rights, royalties, or package benefits outside current authority.

Related Forms and Templates: Hybrid Agreement v1.3.1; JM Signature Agreement v1.0; Publishing Package Addendum.

System Support Reference: Use the governed agreement templates in Implementation HQ.

## 4. How to Issue a Publishing Agreement

Purpose: Prepare the correct agreement for signature.

When to Use: A title has an approved track and is ready for agreement issuance.

Who Owns It: Publishing Coordinator, with Jackie approval for exceptions.

What You Need Before Starting: Approved track, legal author name, book title, effective date, package or relationship details, territory, schedule fields, and any approved addendum terms.

Steps:

1. Confirm the Publishing Track.
2. Use the matching current agreement template from the Forms and Templates index.
3. Fill only existing placeholders.
4. Add the approved package addendum when required.
5. Produce the author-ready file and review it for completeness.
6. Preserve the exact agreement version used.
7. Route for signature only when Jackie has approved the issuance.

Jackie Approval Required? Yes, before issuance and for any exception.

Evidence to Preserve: Agreement version, completed agreement, addendum, approval, storage location, signature status.

What Good Looks Like: The agreement is complete, current, and tied to the approved track.

When to Stop: Stop if a requested change would alter legal language, royalties, package benefits, rights, or term.

Related Forms and Templates: Hybrid Agreement v1.3.1; JM Signature Agreement v1.0; Publishing Package Addendum.

System Support Reference: Use the governed agreement template registry and version register.

## 6. How to Identify the Controlling Manuscript

Purpose: Confirm which manuscript file governs editorial work.

When to Use: Before editing, production preparation, author review, correction, or rework.

Who Owns It: Editorial Lead.

What You Need Before Starting: All manuscript files received, author notes, prior approvals, and current title stage.

Steps:

1. List candidate manuscript files.
2. Identify the newest author-approved or Jackie-approved source.
3. Confirm whether any later file was only reference material.
4. Mark the controlling manuscript in the title record.
5. Preserve older files without treating them as active.
6. Ask for clarification if two files appear equally current.

Jackie Approval Required? Required when the controlling file is disputed or would change scope.

Evidence to Preserve: File name, received date, approval source, decision note.

What Good Looks Like: Editors and production partners work from one known source.

When to Stop: Stop if the source file is missing, corrupted, incomplete, or disputed.

Related Forms and Templates: Manuscript source note; editorial review guide.

System Support Reference: Use the title evidence folder and current work register.

## 7. How to Begin Developmental Editing

When to Use: A manuscript is ready for developmental editing.

Who Owns It: Editorial Lead.

What You Need Before Starting: Controlling manuscript, track, editorial scope, author expectations, deadline, and known sensitivities.

Steps:

1. Confirm developmental editing is approved for the title.
2. Read the manuscript for structure, theme, audience, promise, continuity, and author voice.
3. Prepare notes in plain publishing language.
4. Identify major recommendations and author questions.
5. Preserve the review package.
6. Route sensitive recommendations to Jackie before author delivery.

Jackie Approval Required? Required for major repositioning, sensitive content, or author-facing release.

Evidence to Preserve: Review notes, recommendation package, approval, author-facing version.

What Good Looks Like: The author receives useful guidance without losing their voice.

When to Stop: Stop if the manuscript is not the controlling source or if scope is unclear.

Related Forms and Templates: Editorial Review Guide; Author Review Manuscript.

System Support Reference: Use the current editorial standards and title evidence folder.

## 10. How to Process Author Revisions

When to Use: An author returns edits, comments, approvals, or questions.

Who Owns It: Editorial Lead.

What You Need Before Starting: Author response, prior review package, controlling manuscript, and open question list.

Steps:

1. Preserve the author’s response exactly as received.
2. Identify accepted changes, questions, and disputed items.
3. Update the working manuscript only under the approved editorial path.
4. Prepare unresolved questions for Jackie if needed.
5. Confirm whether another author review is required.
6. Update the title’s next action.

Jackie Approval Required? Required for disputed changes, scope expansion, sensitive content, or final editorial approval.

Evidence to Preserve: Author response, revised manuscript, disposition notes, unresolved questions.

What Good Looks Like: The author’s revisions are honored, tracked, and resolved.

When to Stop: Stop if the author sends a different manuscript without clear instruction.

Related Forms and Templates: Author Review Manuscript; Editorial Review Guide.

System Support Reference: Use the title evidence folder and editorial review references.

## 17. How to Handle a Post-Release Correction

When to Use: A post-release defect, typo, metadata issue, file issue, or author request appears.

Who Owns It: Production Coordinator, with Jackie authority.

What You Need Before Starting: Reported issue, affected editions, current live proof, prior approved source, proposed correction, and impact assessment.

Steps:

1. Identify the issue and affected editions.
2. Preserve the current released state.
3. Ask Jackie whether correction is authorized.
4. Prepare corrected files only for approved affected editions.
5. Verify corrected output before submission.
6. Preserve before-and-after evidence.
7. Confirm corrected live status where applicable.

Jackie Approval Required? Yes.

Evidence to Preserve: Issue report, authorization, prior version, corrected files, verification proof.

What Good Looks Like: The correction is deliberate, traceable, and limited to the approved issue.

When to Stop: Stop if the correction changes title identity, rights, scope, price, author obligations, or an unapproved edition.

Related Forms and Templates: Correction authorization form or record; status update email.

System Support Reference: Use correction authority and title evidence references.

## 18. How to Manage Complimentary Author Copies

Purpose: Fulfill included author-copy benefits correctly.

When to Use: A title reaches the publication point for package copy delivery.

Who Owns It: Author Relations / Communications, with Financial and Royalty Support as needed.

What You Need Before Starting: Package or track, author-copy policy, publication status by edition, shipping details, and any replacement request.

Steps:

1. Confirm the package or track.
2. Confirm the approved and elected Product Forms for the title.
3. Use the current author-copy policy.
4. Confirm the elected edition or delivery form has been published or delivered before promising fulfillment.
5. Record the complimentary entitlement by elected Product Form.
6. Arrange fulfillment through the approved process.
7. Preserve fulfillment proof and author communication.

Plain-English Rule: The package determines how many complimentary print copies are provided. The elected Product Forms determine which print editions receive them. Each elected digital Product Form receives one digital delivery, and an elected audiobook receives one author delivery after publication.

Jackie Approval Required? Required for exceptions, replacements, or additional free copies.

Evidence to Preserve: Policy used, quantities, fulfillment request, delivery proof, communication.

What Good Looks Like: Included copies and digital or audio deliveries match the current policy, the elected Product Forms, and delivery timing.

When to Stop: Stop if the package, elected Product Forms, delivery address, edition status, or exception authority is unclear.

Related Forms and Templates: Complimentary-copy policy; author-copy order support.

System Support Reference: Use PUB-STD: Author Copy Policy.

Steps:

2. Confirm open author, financial, rights, and distribution obligations.
3. Preserve final files and decision evidence.
4. Confirm whether public listings remain, change, or stop.
5. Prepare a closeout summary for Jackie.
6. Communicate only with approved language.

Jackie Approval Required? Yes.

Evidence to Preserve: Closeout decision, final files, obligations, communications, listing status.

What Good Looks Like: A successor can tell whether the title is active, complete, retired, or held and why.

When to Stop: Stop if rights, royalties, public listing, or author expectations are unresolved.

Related Forms and Templates: Title closeout checklist; retirement or reversion policy.

System Support Reference: Use current closeout procedure and title evidence references.
